Edgible for Operations

Back it up, move it, upgrade it, and know when something's wrong, so it keeps running. One playbook so far, in progress: the first three chapters are ready, and the rest are on the way. Every playbook begins with Get Edgible running.

These playbooks are for apps that run from an Edgible card, and work best with a card that says where its data lives. If you built the app yourself, the ideas still apply, but the tools don't, and we haven't tried these steps on a hand-built app.

The chapters:

  • Looking after it: the unglamorous essentials of running your own apps, backed up, restored and proved.
    1. Back up an app
    2. Restore it, and prove it
    3. Move an app to another machine
    4. Upgrade an app safely: coming; for now, see Upgrading, for now
    5. Know when something is wrong: coming
    6. When the machine dies: coming

Upgrading, for now

Every app is different, and we can't predict what an upgrade will ask of yours, so there's no fixed chapter yet. Until there is, the approach is simple, and your AI assistant can do most of it:

  1. Back it up first. Chapter 1. Its fingerprint then tells you whether your data came through.
  2. Ask your AI what the upgrade changes. For example: "What changes if I upgrade my team wiki to the newest version? Anything that could hurt my data?" It can compare the old and new card and tell you in plain words, such as new settings, new image versions, or a database moving to a new major version.
  3. Let it recommend a path, and ask before it acts. That may be the app's usual upgrade, or something specific to the app, such as a database's own upgrade tool. A safe way to try an upgrade is to restore your backup as a copy beside the app and upgrade the copy first (chapters 2 and 3). If the copy breaks, your real app is untouched, and if it works, you can switch. An upgrade can change how your data is stored, and on a copy that costs you nothing.

If the AI isn't sure, it should say so and stop, not guess. We haven't run upgrades end to end in these playbooks, and we may never write one fixed recipe, because apps differ too much.
